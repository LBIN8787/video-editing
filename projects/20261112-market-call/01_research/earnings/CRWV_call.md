## 준비 발언의 핵심 문장 (숫자·약속·위험)
- Margins are rising faster than planned.
- Customer demand remains extremely strong, and our newest contracts are among the most profitable we have ever underwritten.
- And our funding is anchored by secured lending, customer prepayments, and operating cash flows.
- Revenue reached $3.7 billion, up 168% year-over-year and above the high end of our guidance.
- We added more than $1 billion quarter-over-quarter.
- Adjusted operating income reached $379 million, well above our $200 million to $260 million guidance, with margins moving into the low double digits a quarter earlier than planned.
- Revenue backlog grew to approximately $133 billion.
- This quarter, you can see that progress in our results and the guidance we've provided.
- At the end of Q3, our weighted average cost of debt across our entire business was 7.9%, down 250 basis points year-over-year.
- Looking ahead, we will not use the unsecured debt market to fund our business at current yields.
- Consistent with our historical approach, we expect to finance the majority of the remaining CapEx needed to deliver our backlog through delayed draw term loans matched to specific customer contracts.
- Through 2027, more than 75% of committed CapEx is tied to investment-grade and enterprise customers, with the remainder tied to the world's leading AI labs and AI-native companies.
- The pricing of these facilities reflects the underlying customer commitments and our proven ability to deliver.
- They can finance anywhere from 70% to 100% of the associated CapEx.
- Today, that translates to less than 10% at the high end based on current rates.
- As planned, we have already paid down more than $4 billion of debt through this mechanism.
- For the facilities funding long-term commitments, by the time the customer contract ends, the debt is repaid, and the infrastructure is owned free and clear by CoreWeave.
- We also have several other material sources of capital, including prepayments.
- We take an economic approach to prepayments, weighing the impact to pricing against the cost of financing.
- In the past, that has led us to the unsecured debt markets.
- In the current environment, prepayments are more attractive.
- This year, we have collected prepayments on 70% of our deals, with customers funding up to 100% of the contract value upfront.
- In the next three months alone, we will collect approximately $9 billion in prepayments and deposits from signed customer contracts.
- This includes more than $3 billion that relate to recontracting existing cloud infrastructure, which we sold at meaningful premiums to their original price.
- We also expect EBITDA less interest, which excludes the benefit of prepayments, to more than double in 2027, increasing our ability to internally fund capital needs.
- Taken together, we expect these sources to fund the significant majority of our capital needs through 2027, with only a limited amount expected to be raised at the parent company level.
- Beyond our contracted commitments, our success-based CapEx model allows us to adjust the pace of our investment based on customer demand for CoreWeave Cloud and supply constraints, including regulatory impacts and the capital markets.
- Adjusted operating margin was approximately 1% in Q1, 5% in Q2 and exceeded 10% in Q3.
- In July, we increased pricing across SKUs by approximately 25%, followed by another 10% increase later in the quarter.
- Pricing has continued to move higher in Q4, more than offsetting cost increases.
- We are also allocating more capacity to short-term opportunities, where the pricing justifies it, selling capacity at more than $40 million per megawatt annually, while pricing for new infrastructure on four and five-year contracts is moving to $20 million to $25 million per megawatt.
- Short-term contracts let us respond to a strong pricing environment and earn premium returns.
- Strong demand gives us choices in how we best create durable value.
- In Q3, pricing for Amperes and Hoppers increased 30% to 50% quarter-over-quarter, and at prices above their original contracts.
- We are signing multi-year commitments extending beyond the GPU's depreciable lives, driving margins even higher in their later years as price and operating costs remain fixed but depreciation dissipates.
- At current pricing levels, 2027 renewals would generate approaching $4 billion of incremental annualized revenue, with no additional CapEx required.
- This grows to nearly $10 billion of annualized incremental revenue when including 2028 renewals.
- Furthermore, if data center construction across the sector were to slow, installed compute becomes scarcer, and the knock-on effect is that GPU pricing may stay higher for longer.
- The plan rests on contracted demand, success-based CapEx, and the contracts we are already signing.
- In Q3, 70% of the new cloud customers we added were already Forge customers.
- Wayve is one example of how our relationship expands as the work becomes more demanding.
- More than half of our new customers this year came from industries outside AI labs and technology, and 10% of Fortune 500 companies are now CoreWeave customers.
- The number of organizations committing more than $100 million to CoreWeave Cloud increased more than 2.5x year-over-year.
- We have 10x as much qualified demand as available capacity through the end of 2027, and demand for 2028 is already several times larger than our available capacity, even before we have brought it formally to market.
- We continue to expect to reach more than 1.85 gigawatts of active power by year-end.
